Mayor's Decision in Disaster Recovery

A mayor of a flood-prone river city has one budget and three ways to spend it. Disaster risk is Hazard × Exposure × Vulnerability, so shrinking any one of them cuts the losses citizens must recover from. Which one gives the most protection per dollar? Move the sliders and see.

What to notice

  • Small budget? Levees barely help. A half-built levee protects almost no one, so the blue line stays flat at first, then drops steeply once the budget is big enough to finish the job.
  • Turn up the rain. Levees get topped more often, so the blue line loses ground, while buyouts and raised homes keep working. The same rain costs far more when the "older homes" slider is high: the sliders multiply each other.
  • Turn down home value or older homes. Buyouts get cheaper and raising homes matters less, so orange (exposure) can take the lead.
  • Look at the "return per $1" row below. The best choice changes with the city. There is no single right answer, which is why it is a political decision.
